## Phase 0 — Diagnose before you post (Week 1, no filming)

Open **YouTube Studio → Analytics** and answer these before making anything:

1. **Advanced mode → last 90 days → traffic sources.** Which old videos still
   get views, and from where (Search? Suggested? Browse)? Those videos are your
   channel's "pulse" — the topics YouTube still trusts you on.
2. **Audience tab → "Channels your audience watches."** This tells you exactly
   what your 10K care about *now*.
3. **Top videos of all time → sort by average view duration %,** not views.
   Your best retention formats are the templates for comeback content.
4. Write down your channel's realistic baseline: median views per video over
   the last 10 uploads. That's the number the revival is measured against.

## Phase 1 — The rebrand trap (read this before renaming anything)

**Do not scrub the disc golf identity off the channel.** Your subscribers and
your algorithmic authority are both disc-golf-shaped. A channel that suddenly
posts generic "media production" content to a disc golf audience will see CTR
collapse, YouTube will stop recommending, and the 10K becomes dead weight.

The play that works:

- **Channel stays audience-first:** disc golf remains the flagship content
  pillar (~70% of uploads). The channel is where fans live.
- **Top World Media is the brand behind it:** rename to "Top World Media"
  is fine *if* the banner, trailer, and About text immediately say
  "home of Top World Disc Golf" — continuity must be explicit.
- **Client/commercial work lives on topworldmedia.com** as a portfolio, not
  as channel uploads. The channel *sells* the production business by being
  living proof of it (add the site link to every description).
- The remaining ~30% of uploads: behind-the-scenes production content —
  "how we film a tournament," gear breakdowns, edit breakdowns. This serves
  disc golf fans AND markets the studio. It's the bridge between the old
  brand and the new one.

      email for production inquiries.

## Phase 3 — The content engine (Weeks 2–12)

**Cadence that revives channels: 1 long-form + 3 Shorts per week.**
Sustainable beats ambitious. Every long-form shoot gets cut into Shorts —
you're filming once and publishing four times.

Content pillars (rotate weekly):

| Pillar | Long-form | Shorts pulled from it |
|---|---|---|
| Tournament coverage | Event recap / final-round breakdown | Best 3 throws, crowd reactions |
| Player interviews | 15–20 min sit-down | The single spiciest answer, 45s |
| Course tours | Full walkthrough | "This hole is unfair" clip |
| Production BTS | How we filmed X | Gear-in-bag, before/after edit |

**Packaging rules (this is where dormant channels lose):**

- Title + thumbnail decided **before** filming, not after.
- Thumbnails: one face or one disc in flight, max 4 words, brand red as the
  only accent color. Consistent style = channel recognition in the feed.
- Titles lead with the tension, not the event name:
  "This putt decided $10,000" beats "MVP Open Final Round Recap."
- Ride search spikes: player names and tournament names in titles/descriptions
  the week the event happens — that's when the world searches for them.

**Retention rules:**

- First 30 seconds: cold-open the best moment, then context. Never an intro.
- End screens always point to one specific video, not "my channel."

## Phase 4 — Compounding (ongoing)

- **Reply to every comment in the first 2 hours** after upload — early
  engagement velocity is the strongest reviving signal you control.
- **Collab with players and mid-size disc golf channels** — a guest spot in
  each direction per month. Borrowed audiences are the fastest way to reach
  people who forgot you.
- **Cross-post Shorts to TikTok and Reels** with native captions (don't leave
  watermarks on). Same edit, three platforms, three chances.
- **Pin a production-services comment** on BTS videos — that's the funnel from
  channel to paying clients.

## Scoreboard — 30/60/90 day targets

| Metric (YouTube Studio) | 30 days | 60 days | 90 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Uploads kept on schedule | 4 long + 12 Shorts | 8 + 24 | 12 + 36 |
| Click-through rate (long-form) | ≥ 3% | ≥ 4% | ≥ 5% |
| Avg. % viewed (long-form) | ≥ 35% | ≥ 40% | ≥ 45% |
| Returning viewers trend | flat → up | up | up |
| Median views vs. pre-revival baseline | +25% | +75% | 2× |

If CTR misses two videos in a row → change thumbnails first, topic second.
If retention misses → cut the first minute harder. One variable at a time.
